# Design Rules & Core UI/UX Principles

> **Core Philosophy**: A clean, balanced, and purposeful design system that emphasizes clarity, strong visual hierarchy, and breathing room over superficial decoration.

---

## 1. Keep It Simple
What’s the point of having too many effects and styles, if your aim is to create a clean design? Too many effects, transitions, and colours can mess up your design and you won’t be able to scan your content in the future.
- Avoid unnecessary decorative clutter and redundant visual elements.
- Use transitions and animations sparingly to enhance comprehension, not to distract.
- Restrict palettes to purposeful, harmonious tones that serve content clarity.

---

## 2. Balance and Alignment
Never forget, everything you are putting on a page has some weight. Weight can be determined by colour, size, or texture. Without moderation, your audience will feel as if their eye is popping out of their page.
- Distribute visual weight evenly across layout axes.
- Maintain consistent grid lines and strict alignment for text, icons, and containers.
- Balance heavy focal points (e.g., imagery, bold titles) with quiet, grounded surfaces.

---

## 3. White Space (Negative Space)
All other design principles are related to what you add to your design. White space or negative space is the only one that is directly responsible for what you do not add. The white space is the same as a blank page around the element in your design.
- Give content room to breathe; generous margins and paddings elevate perceived quality and luxury.
- Prevent cramped containers and overlapping visual hierarchies.
- Use whitespace intentionally to group related items and separate distinct sections.

---

## 4. Be Consistent
Consistent UI means using similar design patterns, identical terminology in prompts, homogeneous menus and screens, and consistent command throughout the interface.
- Standardize interactive states (hover, active, focus, disabled).
- Re-use established design tokens (borders, shadows, radiuses, colors).
- Use uniform terminology across all labels, tooltips, and action dispatches.

---

## 5. Typography System
Create tidiness and structure with consistent rules for font size. Just like weights, a small difference in size between two types of type may distort contrast and hierarchy. Their rules will quickly blend into each other.
- Enforce strict type scales with defined step intervals.
- Do not introduce arbitrary, near-identical font sizes that blur structural contrast.
- Harmonize font weights (regular, medium, bold) to clearly distinguish labels from body copy.

---

## 6. Make Readable Titles
It is important to have a really strong hierarchy between your elements. This way, you can guide the user’s eye from the most important things to the least important ones.
- Headlines must be distinct, scannable, and instantly readable.
- Maintain sufficient color contrast between text and background surfaces.
- Avoid small, dense, or scattered text blocks in title zones.

---

## 7. Hierarchy
Strong visuals hierarchy is a core design principle of a successful user interface. It consists of arranging visual elements in a way that explains the level of importance of each element and guides the user to take the desired actions.
- Anchor pages with a clear primary focal point.
- Guide the viewer's journey through progressive disclosure: Primary action → Secondary context → Tertiary metadata.
- Ensure that the most critical user actions (Search, Contact, Filter, Save) stand out effortlessly without visual friction.
